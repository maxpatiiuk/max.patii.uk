What started as a way to improve my English listening skills has turned into an
addictive hobby. 8 years later, I crossed
[1,000 books](https://www.goodreads.com/review/list/83169518-max-patiiuk?shelf=read).

Let me share the most memorable books and what I learned along the way.

## Fiction

### LitRPG

LitRPG is a genre that combines RPG (role-playing game) elements with a fantasy
story in a book form. Listening to these books feels like watching a YouTube
video game playthrough. Unlike a typical YouTube video, though, an audiobook has
much higher production value, including professional editing and narration.

The best part is that LitRPG is usually set in a hyper-advanced virtual reality
world or a gamified alternate universe. Unlike any video game, a LitRPG world
can contain sentient NPCs, infinite skill trees, and nation-scale politics.

The RPG elements are the most enjoyable part. The best LitRPG books give a sense
of continuous progression. Some focus on character upskilling and crafting.
Others focus on world-building and politics - from a small tribe to an empire.

Lazy authors jump straight from city-level politics to "save the universe"
stuff, as in a cheap superhero movie - an extreme case of scope creep. Then the
publisher says, "Write one more book," and the author is forced to do a soft
reset - a serious immersion killer.

Where as, the best stories stretch across thousands of pages of paced
progression and world-building.

My favorites:

- [CivCEO by Andrew Karevik](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/57034114-civceo) -
  an awesome use of economics and politics. I binged the entire series in 2
  days.
- [Drone Ensign by Kyle Johnson](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/58711513-drone-ensign) -
  a journey from the slave class to an admiral in a post-scarcity sci-fi
  society. It features a hyper-competent, rational protagonist - my favorite
  kind of character.
- [Portal to Nova Roma by J.R. Mathews](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/60288155-portal-to-nova-roma) -
  an alternate-history Roman Empire with a hyper-competent, rational
  protagonist. Slow-burn empire-building.
- [Life Reset by Shemer Kuznits](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/35962125-life-reset) -
  highly focused city-building with a competent protagonist playing as a goblin,
  of all things.
- [He Who Fights with Monsters by Shirtaloon](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/57189884-he-who-fights-with-monsters) -
  great humor, great pacing, many pages.

Honorable mentions:

- [Fostering Faust by Randi Darren](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/40059988-fostering-faust) -
  a refreshing tale of an "immoral" hero.

  from a lone hustler to a multiverse-spanning empire. A cold, competent
  protagonist and likable characters, but too much scope inflation followed by
  too many soft resets.

### Science fiction

- [Honor Harrington by David Weber](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/35921.On_Basilisk_Station) -
  a 40-book series written over 23 years. It is one of the longest space-opera
  series, with extensive sci-fi politics - I binged it over several months.
- [Daemon by Daniel Suarez](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/6665847-daemon) -
  replacing the old world order with the power of code.
- [The Calculating Stars by Mary Robinette Kowal](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/33080122-the-calculating-stars) -
  an alternate history in which the space race accelerated rather than stopped.
- [Old Man's War by John Scalzi](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/36510196-old-man-s-war) -
  classic military sci-fi and politics at the interstellar scale.
- [Just One Damned Thing After Another by Jodi Taylor](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/29661618-just-one-damned-thing-after-another) -
  a long series full of British humor that made me laugh out loud.
- [The Martian by Andy Weir](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/18007564-the-martian) -
  masterful hard sci-fi.
- [Ender's Shadow by Orson Scott Card](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/9532.Ender_s_Shadow) -
  a story of hyper-talented children engaging in world-scale politics and war.

### Classic science fiction

- [Parable of the Sower by Octavia E. Butler](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/52397.Parable_of_the_Sower) -

## Non-fiction

### The 1000th book

My 1,000th book was the nearly 1,000-page
[JFK: Coming of Age in the American Century, 1917–1956 by Fredrik Logevall](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/49350179-jfk).
To my surprise, you can fill a thick book with JFK's biography before even
reaching his presidency.

### Politics

My favorite kind of book in this genre covers both sides of an argument fairly
and then advocates for a better middle ground.

- [Breakneck: China's Quest to Engineer the Future by Dan Wang](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/223736214-breakneck) -
  despite the title, the book has valuable things to say about both the US (a
  lawyer-led society) and China (an engineer-led society), advocating for a
  better middle ground.
- [The Narrow Corridor: States, Societies, and the Fate of Liberty by Daron Acemoglu](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/52884091-the-narrow-corridor) -
  studies the balance between state and society, with many historical and recent
  examples.
- [What's Our Problem?: A Self-Help Book for Societies by Tim Urban](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/102146148-what-s-our-problem) -
  an honest analysis of the left and right in modern US politics (as well as up
  and down).
- [How to Hide an Empire: A History of the Greater United States by Daniel Immerwahr](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/40121985-how-to-hide-an-empire) -
  an engaging history of US territorial expansion and colonies, extending into
  the modern day.
- [The Perfect Police State: An Undercover Odyssey into China's Terrifying Surveillance Dystopia of the Future by Geoffrey Cain](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/55277916-the-perfect-police-state) -
  the surveillance technostate described in the book is far more efficient and
  omnipresent than even Orwell could have imagined.
- [Atlas Shrugged by Ayn Rand](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/662.Atlas_Shrugged) -
  not strictly non-fiction, but very influential. Its black-and-white approach
  is unrealistic, but I appreciate how the book teaches you to think rationally
  rather than with your gut, demand fair compensation for your contributions,
  and not expect rewards unless you create value.

![How to Hide an Empire by Daniel Immerwahr](../../../public/articles/1000-books/how-to-hide-an-empire.avif)

### Anthropology

- [Humankind: A Hopeful History by Rutger Bregman](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/52879286-humankind) -
  an optimistic account of human history - first time you read one of these, it
  changes you worldview.
- [Factfulness: Ten Reasons We're Wrong About the World – and Why Things Are Better Than You Think by Hans Rosling](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/34890015-factfulness) -
  another optimistic account of human history - they work well together.
- [Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind by Yuval Noah Harari](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/23692271-sapiens) -
  the author offers a unique perspective on human affairs.
- [Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies by Nick Bostrom](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/20527133-superintelligence) -
  an instant classic, still relevant.
- [Life 3.0: Being Human in the Age of Artificial Intelligence by Max Tegmark](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/34272565-life-3-0) -
  an accessible book on AI's impact on humanity.

![Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies by Nick Bostrom](../../../public/articles/1000-books/superintelligence.avif)

### Macroeconomics

- [Why Economies Rise or Fall by Peter Rodriguez](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/8194768-why-economies-rise-or-fall) -
  the most important book on economics I've ever read. It introduces practical
  concepts and applies them to the modern world rather than focusing on dry
  theory. Left/right politics, incentives, and globalization.
- [Freakonomics by Steven D. Levitt](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/1202.Freakonomics) -
  a fun pop-economics book that makes you look differently at the world.

![Freakonomics by Steven D. Levitt](../../../public/articles/1000-books/freakonomics.avif)

### Finance

- [Elon Musk: Tesla, SpaceX, and the Quest for a Fantastic Future by Ashlee Vance](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/25541028-elon-musk) -

- [Tim Cook: The Genius Who Took Apple to the Next Level by Leander Kahney](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/40909435-tim-cook) -

### Biohacking and self-improvement

- [The Body: A Guide for Occupants by Bill Bryson](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/43582376-the-body) -
  a well-rounded book about the human body.
- [Be Obsessed or Be Average by Grant Cardone](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/27246148-be-obsessed-or-be-average) -
  this is a bit of a pulp non-fiction book, but I found it very motivating.
- [Whole: Rethinking the Science of Nutrition by T. Colin Campbell](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/13587039-whole) -
  advocates for eating cleaner and choosing less-processed food, though the book

- [Lifespan: Why We Age―and Why We Don't Have To by David A. Sinclair](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/43723901-lifespan) -
  an introduction to longevity research.
- [The Case Against Sugar by Gary Taubes](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/29874881-the-case-against-sugar) -
  the start of my zero-added-sugar diet.

## Q&A

### How much do you remember?

The books I liked most are the ones I remember best.

With fiction books, I remember the high-level plot and the best moments. Just
reading a book's description is enough to bring back more than you might expect.

With non-fiction, the exact details are lost even before you finish the book.
But the best books give you mental models and practical concepts that stay with
you even after you forget where they came from. These are the books that change
your behavior not fully consciously - the ones you quote while speaking to a
friend without realizing it.
